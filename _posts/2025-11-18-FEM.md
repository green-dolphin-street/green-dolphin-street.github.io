* **Finite Element Analysis (FEA):** A detailed engineering simulation method.
    * **Goal:** To accurately predict how an object will internally deform, bend, or break under real-world forces (like stress, heat, or vibration).
    * **How it works:** It divides a 3D model into a **volumetric mesh** (many small, solid pieces called "elements").
    * **Physics:** It applies complex physics equations to *every single element* to calculate its internal stress and strain.
    * **Focus:** **Accuracy**. Answers "Will this material fail?"
    * **Analogy:** A digital car crash *investigation*, analyzing exactly how the metal crumples.

* **Game Engine Physics (Rigid Body):** A simplified physics system for real-time entertainment.
    * **Goal:** To create visually believable motion and collisions quickly (in real-time).
    * **How it works:** It uses a **surface mesh** (a "collider," which is hollow) to represent the object.
    * **Physics:** It treats the *entire object* as one single, undeformable "rigid body" and calculates its overall motion, rotation, and bouncing.
    * **Focus:** **Speed**. Answers "Where did this object move?"
    * **Analogy:** A digital car crash *stunt*, making the car tumble realistically but not analyzing the metal.

* **Key Comparison:**
    * FEA calculates physics *inside* a solid mesh to see if it **breaks**.
    * Game Physics calculates physics *on* a hollow mesh to see how it **moves**.
