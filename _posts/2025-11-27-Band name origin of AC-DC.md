### Origin Story
The name AC/DC was spotted on a sewing machine power adapter by Margaret Young, the sister of founding members Malcolm and Angus Young. She suggested it to her brothers, who felt it perfectly captured the high-energy, electrifying nature of their music.

#### Did that sewing machine really handle both AC and DC power?
Yes! Many sewing machines are designed to operate on both Alternating Current (AC) and Direct Current (DC) power sources, making them versatile for different electrical environments. The AC/DC label on the adapter indicated this dual compatibility.

#### Did it have separate power adapters for AC and DC?
No, the sewing machine typically used a single power adapter that could accept both AC and DC inputs, converting them to the appropriate voltage and current needed for the machine's operation. The technology for this dual compatibility is called a universal power supply, which contain circuits that can rectify AC to DC and regulate voltage for both types of input. They often include:
* **Rectifier Circuits:** Convert AC to DC using diodes.
* **Voltage Regulators:** Maintain a constant output voltage regardless of input variations.
* **Filtering Components:** Smooth out the DC output to ensure stable operation of the sewing machine.

The specific brand and model of the sewing machine that inspired the AC/DC name have not been publicly documented. ### Band Symbolism
* **Energy:** The Young brothers felt the name represented the band's raw power and high-voltage energy.
* **Style:** It fit their driving, "electrifying" musical style.

### Trivia
* **Australian Nickname:** Fans in Australia often refer to the band as "Acca Dacca."
* **Myth Busting:** The band has repeatedly denied rumors that the name stands for "Anti-Christ/Devil's Child."
