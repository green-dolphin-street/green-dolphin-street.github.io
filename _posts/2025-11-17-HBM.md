High bandwidth memory (HBM) is a type of **DRAM** that achieves significantly higher bandwidth and lower power consumption than traditional memory standards like DDR or GDDR.

---

* **Core Technology**
    * **3D Stacking**: Multiple DRAM dies (up to 8 or 12) are stacked vertically to form a single memory "cube."
    * **Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs)**: Vertical electrical connections that pass *through* the silicon dies, linking the stacked chips together.
    * **Logic Base Die**: A foundational chip at the bottom of the stack that handles control and I/O.
    * **Wide Interface**: Utilizes an extremely wide data path (1024 bits or more per stack) operating at a lower clock speed, prioritizing parallel data transfer.

---

* **Packaging**
    * **2.5D Integration**: The HBM stack is placed next to the main processor (CPU/GPU) on a silicon **interposer**.
    * **Interposer**: A high-density wiring substrate that acts as a short, high-speed bridge between the processor and the HBM stack.

---

* **Key Characteristics**
    * **Bandwidth**: Massive data throughput (measured in hundreds of GB/s to TB/s).
    * **Power Efficiency**: Low voltage operation and short signal paths reduce energy consumption per bit.
    * **Form Factor**: The vertical stacking yields a much smaller footprint (area) on the circuit board.

---

* **Generations (JEDEC Standards)**
    * **HBM1**: Initial version (1.0 Gbps).
    * **HBM2/HBM2E**: Increased capacity and speed (up to 3.6 Gbps).
    * **HBM3/HBM3E**: Latest versions, featuring doubled channel count and highest speeds (up to 8.0 Gbps).
